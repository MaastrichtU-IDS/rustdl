# v0.4.19 — the per-pair default drops 1000 ms → 5 ms

**16 ORE recoveries, 0 regressions, −15.9% corpus wall.** Two defaults change, and they
are coupled: do not flip one without the other.

## The change

| default | was | now | revert |
|---|---|---|---|
| `classify --pair-timeout-ms` | 1000 | **5** | `--pair-timeout-ms 1000` |
| `RUSTDL_CLASSIFY_PROBE_ON_INCOMPLETE` | (new, off) | **on** | `=0` |

Only `classify` moved. `disjoint`, `individuals` and `property-values` keep their own
1000 ms defaults — the sweep measured `classify`, and flipping the others would be
unmeasured. Pinned by `pair_timeout_default.rs`.

**Why they are coupled.** A 5 ms budget times out the per-class unsat probes, each of
which then defaults to *satisfiable* — sound for subsumption, but it empties
`unsatisfiable_idxs`, which is the classify inconsistency detector's admission signal.
Ship the budget alone and `ore_ont_16372` reports `consistent: true` on an ontology
Konclude, HermiT and rustdl's own `consistent` all call inconsistent. The second flag
admits the probe on an incomplete `ABox`-bearing run, restoring the verdict at a cost of
**+0.2 s** (the probe's own 200 ms budget).

## The two "regressions" were cap-boundary artifacts, and it took repeats to know

The sweep flagged `ore_ont_7204` (55.4 s → dnf) and `ore_ont_9674` (58.7 s → dnf). Three
repeats per arm:

| ontology | v0.4.18 | v0.4.19 |
|---|---|---|
| `ore_ont_9674` | 57.6, 59.7, 59.0 | **58.2, 57.8, 58.1** — completes 3/3, *faster* |
| `ore_ont_7204` | 57.1, 53.8, 51.8 | 54.3, 59.8, 60.1/DNF |

`ore_ont_9674` is not a regression at all. `ore_ont_7204` straddles the 60 s cap in
**both** arms and is ~3 s slower here — genuinely marginal, not cleanly broken.

**This exposes a limit in the gate.** A single-run sweep at a 60 s cap cannot distinguish
"regressed" from "always ran at 58 ± 2 s"; both flagged ontologies live in that band, and
only re-running them told us which was which. **Treat any `ok → dnf` whose baseline wall
is within ~10% of the cap as unresolved until repeated.** `ore_ont_7204` and the three
v0.4.18 marginals (`868`, `9674`, `10689`, all 56.8–58.7 s) will flip on a slower host.

## Why 1000 ms was wrong

It was "the empirical knee on pizza" — tuned on one ontology. On the hard tail the
engines never terminate on a pair: they burn the whole budget and time out having found
nothing, so a large budget buys no completeness and *starves* the phases that do the
work. `ore_ont_934` spent ~108 s in `unsat_probe` (≈ 108 classes × 1 s) and never reached
`tier_walk` at all; at 5 ms it classifies in 50 s with FP=0/MISSED=0 against an
adjudicated Konclude oracle.

## Freshness canary

`wine` at the **default** is now **~2.7 s** (197 subsumptions). History, since each step
is a different binary and mixing them misreads a change as staleness: v0.4.13 ~74 s
unbounded, v0.4.18 ~38 s, v0.4.19 **~2.7 s at the default** — because the default budget
is now 5 ms. A wine near ~38 s means a pre-v0.4.19 binary; near ~74 s, pre-v0.4.18.

## Upgrade note

Results may be **less complete** than v0.4.18 on ontologies with genuinely hard pairs —
that is the trade, it is bounded at ΔMISSED +1.04% on the measured population, and it is
never unsound (a truncated pair defaults to *not subsumed*). Runs that lose a pair print
a prominent `INCOMPLETE` warning. For maximum completeness use
`--pair-timeout-ms 1000` (prior behaviour) or `--pair-timeout-ms 0` (unbounded).
